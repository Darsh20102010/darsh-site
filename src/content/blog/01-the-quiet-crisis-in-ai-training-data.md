Imagine training a student using photocopies of textbooks, then photocopies of those photocopies-until the original text slowly fades. The student keeps studying, yet understanding weakens. This is the general trend increasingly being observed in modern AI systems.

Large AI models rely heavily on publicly available data in diverse forms: articles, forums, media, human activities etc. Much of this high quality data has already been used repeatedly. Moreover a large chunk of new online content is generated by AI itself. For example, security researchers have shown that tools like GitHub Copilot can repeat insecure or outdated code patterns already present in repositories, effectively amplifying existing mistakes. When models train on recycled or synthetic data, small errors compound, diversity drops, and reliability suffers—a process researchers call model collapse. Bigger models cannot fix weaker learning signals.

The solution is therefore better learning habits: curated datasets, human-in-the-loop training, and tighter data pipelines. The future of AI may depend less on how much it learns and more on what it learns from. If machines keep studying copies instead of originals, intelligence rather than moving forward, will circle back to itself.

> “Where is the wisdom we have lost in knowledge? Where is the knowledge we have lost in information?” - T.S Eliot
